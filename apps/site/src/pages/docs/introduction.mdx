easy-cookie is an open-source (MIT) and self-hosted cookie consent solution. It replaces the parts of Iubenda that a small or medium site needs: the banner with its preferences panel, prior blocking of scripts, Google Consent Mode v2, the consent register, the cookie policy tables and the legal documents that go with them.

There is no third-party service in the middle. The code runs in your visitors' browsers and, if you want proof of consent, on your own server.

## One config, five outputs

Everything starts from a single configuration object, in plain JSON. You declare categories, services and cookies once, and easy-cookie derives:

| From `config` | Output |
|---|---|
| `categories`, `texts` | The **banner** and the preferences panel, in six languages |
| `categories`, `services` | **Prior blocking** of scripts and iframes, and cookie cleanup when consent is withdrawn |
| `categories[].gcm` | The **Google Consent Mode v2** states: the `default` in `<head>` and every `update` |
| `revision`, `log` | An entry in the **consent register** for every choice, with the revision that was accepted |
| `services[].cookies` | The **cookie table** of the cookie policy |

Because there is only one source, what the visitor reads in the banner, what gets blocked and what the policy says cannot drift apart.

You don't have to write the services by hand: the [service library](/docs/service-library/) ships about thirty ready-made declarations (GA4, Meta Pixel, YouTube, Stripe and more) that you copy into the config and edit.

## The packages

Every package is published on npm under the `@easy-cookie` scope, and they all share one version number.

| Package | What it does |
|---|---|
| `@easy-cookie/core` | The browser runtime: banner, preferences, blocking, Consent Mode, sending records. ESM and `<script>` (IIFE) builds plus the stylesheet. Also `@easy-cookie/core/shared` (config types, validation, head snippet, server-side cookie reader) and `@easy-cookie/core/catalog` (the service library). |
| `@easy-cookie/react` | Provider, `useConsent()` hook, and components that load scripts and iframes after consent. |
| `@easy-cookie/next` | The Consent Mode snippet in `<head>`, consent checks on the server, and published legal documents and config fetched for Server Components, for the Next.js App Router. |
| `@easy-cookie/policy` | Cookie policy tables (HTML, Markdown or React) from the same config, and the Markdown renderer for legal documents. |
| `@easy-cookie/posthog` | Keeps PostHog in sync with consent, with cookieless mode after a rejection. |

Next to the packages, the repository contains the **consent server** (Node and Postgres) and its **admin console**. The server hosts the published config, keeps the consent register and serves the legal documents; the console is where you edit the banner, add services from the library, write the policies and search the register. See [Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting/) and [Admin console](/docs/console/).

## The rules you cannot get wrong

Some rules from the Italian data protection authority's 2021 cookie guidelines and from the EDPB cookie banner taskforce are not options: they are built into the banner and integrators cannot turn them off.

- **Accept** and **Reject** carry the same weight: two equal halves of one row.
- The **X closes the banner and counts as a rejection**, recorded as `close` in the register.
- Non-necessary categories start **off**. Scrolling the page never counts as consent.
- The banner **comes back** when you change `revision`, or after `expiryDays` (182 days, about 6 months).
- A **button to reopen the preferences** is always available, and withdrawing is as easy as accepting.

The point-by-point checklist is on the [Compliance](/docs/compliance/) page.

## What it doesn't do

- It does not implement IAB TCF, non-EU regimes or geolocation.
- It does not scan your site for cookies, and it does not block known domains automatically. Blocking is declared in the markup, as with Iubenda's manual tags.
- It does not generate legal texts for you. The console ships editable Italian and English **drafts** of a cookie policy, a privacy policy and terms, with every owner-specific value left as a placeholder. They are a starting point to be completed and reviewed by a professional, not legal advice. See [Legal documents](/docs/legal-documents/).
